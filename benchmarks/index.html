What can AI infer from a nickel complex’s geometry?

I designed three tasks that ask for different kinds of chemical judgement. In the first two, a model proposes atomic coordinates before an independent single-point calculation scores them. In the third, the model receives coordinates and predicts an energy ordering. The results below describe individual runs on this family of nickel complexes.

Test 1 · Predict a relaxed geometry

Starting from a seed structure, the model constructed a candidate using geometric operations. I evaluated its submitted coordinates with a gas-phase ORCA B97-3c single-point calculation. The energy gap measures how the candidate compares with the historical reference geometry under that scoring method.

Does a molecular viewer help?

In a separate experiment, GPT-6.1 Sol Max made one prediction under each of three viewing conditions for the same 99-atom seed. After alignment with the reference, all-atom RMSD was 1.046 Å without tools, 2.335 Å with the ball-and-stick viewer, and 1.070 Å with added orbital schematics. The seed itself was 1.105 Å from the reference.

The ball-and-stick submission placed one P–Ni–O angle within 0.4° of its reference value while introducing the largest error across the molecule. This is why the benchmark records local coordination and whole-structure agreement separately.

Test 2 · Predict a ligand-dissociation product

The model had to choose a departing ligand and supply coordinates for the remaining complex. GPT-6.2 Astra Max proposed phosphine dissociation with CO retained. Its 57-atom product scored 4.91 kcal/mol above the reference for that branch at gas-phase B97-3c.

The alternative dissociation branch has a different molecular composition. Each product therefore needs its own reference. This test evaluates a proposed product geometry and leaves the relative rates of dissociation unresolved.

Test 4 · Rank structures from coordinates

Models ranked 17 candidate structures. Historical ORCA single-point energies were available for 13, giving 78 scorable pairs. A correctly ordered pair earned one point and a tie earned half a point.

Models could order many pairs correctly and still miss the lowest-energy candidate. These pairs share structures, and the task was reused. Eight reference energies had raw-output support and five were traceable only to historical reports.

Two additional owner-supplied submissions, Claude Fable 5.2 and Claude Opus 5.5 (High), scored 87.18% and 73.72% respectively. Fable selected the lowest scored structure. Only final responses were available for these two submissions, and both reported seeing directory-name hints.

Revised protocol

The Refined-12 edition removes four unscored entries and one near-duplicate. It requires a strict ordering of 12 candidates, giving 66 pairs. The scores above belong to the original edition.

Benchmark tools

Gaussian Bench prepares geometry-prediction prompts from molecular structures and compares submissions with private references using explicit atom mappings. Molkit supports controlled tool-access experiments by exposing distances, angles and ligand orientation through Python and a molecular viewer.
