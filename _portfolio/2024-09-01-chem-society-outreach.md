I co-founded the **Xi'an Jiaotong-Liverpool University Chemistry Society**
and served as its Vice President. Beyond running the society's internal
activities, I led its public-facing outreach work: organising chemistry
popularisation lectures and designing a chemistry-themed board game
intended to explain reaction mechanisms through play rather than rote
memorisation.

## What I did

The board game was the centrepiece. I designed it around actual organic
reaction mechanisms, so that players advance by choosing reasonable
reaction pathways and recognising functional-group behaviour — the rules
reinforce the same logic a student needs in a mechanism course, but in a
form that works for a general audience with no prior chemistry
background.

## What I learned

The exercise forced me to separate *what is true* from *what is
explainable*. A mechanism that is correct on paper can still be unusable
as a game rule if the underlying concept has no intuitive handle. Safety
considerations also had to be built in from the outset rather than added
as an afterthought, which is a discipline I now carry into laboratory
work. Running the lectures and the game together taught me how to adjust
an explanation on the fly when the audience's level turns out to be
different from what I expected.
