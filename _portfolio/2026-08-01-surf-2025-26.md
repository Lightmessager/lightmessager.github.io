In the **Summer Undergraduate Research Fellowship 2025–26** I worked
under Dr Yue Zhang on the computational study of a nickel-catalysed
five-membered ring-closing reaction. My work focused on locating the
ring-closing transition state and characterising the geometry of the
pathway.

## Computational approach

- Geometry optimisations and transition-state searches were carried out
  with Gaussian, using a constrained-bond scan as the initial guess for
  the forming C–C bond.
- The TS geometry was reoptimised without the constraint and confirmed
  by frequency analysis: one imaginary frequency, with the corresponding
  normal mode aligned with the reaction coordinate.
- IRC calculations were run in both directions to verify that the
  transition state connects the intended cyclisation precursor and the
  five-membered product.
- Solvent effects were treated with the SMD model.

## What the calculations showed

The ring closure proceeds through a concerted C–C bond-forming transition
state. The forming bond distance at the TS is consistent with a
late transition state, which agrees with the experimental kinetic
isotope effect reported for this system. The five-membered ring adopts
a half-chair-like geometry in the product, and the TS geometry retains
much of that ring conformation.

## My role

I was responsible for the majority of the transition-state searches on
the five-membered cyclisation system, including the initial bond-length
scan, the reoptimisation and the IRC verification. This involved
adjusting the bond length and conformational parameters of the ring
precursor close to thirty times before a geometry was found that
converged to the correct stationary point and produced a single
imaginary frequency. The experience made the difference between a
plausible guess and a verified pathway clear to me: a TS is not
established until the IRC connects it to both sides.
