**SURF 2024–25 · Xi'an Jiaotong-Liverpool University**

**Supervisor: Dr Xiaotai Wang**

## Research Question

Which candidate elementary steps provide a plausible mechanism for the nickel/zinc-mediated reductive coupling of heteroaryl bromides with tertiary alkyl halides?

## Chemical System

This project concerned reductive cross-electrophile coupling involving nickel and zinc. I considered oxidative-addition, single-electron-transfer (SET) and concerted mechanistic proposals.

![Schematic showing the three candidate pathway families considered in the coupling project]({{ '/images/research/coupling-pathways.svg' | relative_url }})

*Candidate pathway families only; arrows do not specify elementary steps, barrier heights or a preferred mechanism.*

## Computational Approach

I used Gaussian for geometry optimisation, transition-state searches and energy processing, and assembled energetic cycles to compare candidate pathways. Exact functional, basis-set, effective-core-potential and solvation settings are not specified in this public summary.

## My Contribution

- Proposed and investigated three candidate pathway families and assembled their energetic cycles.
- Performed geometry optimisations and transition-state searches, including repeated refinement of an oxidative-addition transition-state guess.
- Processed calculation outputs and prepared plots for comparison of reaction pathways.
- Undertook related cobalt-system calculations based on a published Co/Ni cross-coupling study to develop familiarity with the computational workflow.

## Mechanistic Challenge

Locating the oxidative-addition transition state required repeated adjustments to the starting geometry. I also learned to diagnose methyl-group oscillation during optimisation. These tasks helped me distinguish numerical convergence problems from questions about the chemical model.

## Results and Validation

The project gave me experience carrying a mechanistic calculation from molecular models to an energetic cycle. This public summary does not assign a preferred pathway or report quantitative barriers.

## Output

This work forms part of my undergraduate research experience; no journal publication is claimed here.
