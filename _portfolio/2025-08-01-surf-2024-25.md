During the **Summer Undergraduate Research Fellowship 2024–25** I worked
under Dr Haifeng Yang on the mechanism of nickel / zinc-catalysed
reductive cross-coupling between heteroaryl bromides and tertiary alkyl
halides. My responsibility was the computational side of the project:
geometry optimisations and transition-state searches using Gaussian.

## Computational details

- Level of theory: B3LYP/6-31G(d) with SMD implicit solvation
  (THF, dielectric 7.43), unless stated otherwise.
- Solvent screening: a short solvent scan was carried out over toluene,
  THF, DMF and acetonitrile.
- A constrained scan of the C–C forming bond was used to locate the
  transition state; the geometry was then reoptimised without the
  constraint and verified by frequency analysis (one imaginary frequency
  aligned with the reaction coordinate).
- IRC calculations were run to confirm that the TS connects the intended
  reactant and product.

## Mechanistic conclusions

Three pathways were evaluated:

1. **Oxidative addition** of the aryl bromide to Ni(0), followed by
   radical capture of the tertiary alkyl halide and reductive elimination.
2. **Single-electron transfer** from a Ni(0) or Ni(I) species to the
   alkyl halide, generating an alkyl radical that then recombines.
3. A **concerted** three-component pathway in which the organozinc reagent
   and the two electrophiles participate simultaneously.

The barrier computed for the oxidative-addition route was markedly lower
than those for the SET and concerted alternatives, which is consistent
with the experimental observation that the coupling proceeds under
mild conditions and with broad functional-group tolerance.

## My role

I carried out the majority of the geometry optimisations and transition
state searches for the oxidative-addition and concerted pathways,
including the constrained-bond scan and the IRC verification. The
solvent-screening calculations were performed in collaboration with other
members of the group.
