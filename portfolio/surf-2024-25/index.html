Ni/Zn Reductive Cross-Electrophile Coupling

SURF 2024–25 · Xi'an Jiaotong-Liverpool University
Supervisor: Dr Xiaotai Wang
Research with Yanran Yang

Research Question

Which candidate elementary steps provide plausible mechanistic routes for the nickel/zinc-mediated reductive coupling of heteroaryl bromides with tertiary alkyl halides?

Chemical System

This project concerned reductive cross-electrophile coupling involving nickel and zinc. The team compared oxidative-addition, single-electron-transfer (SET) and SN2-type mechanistic proposals. I also carried out related cobalt-system calculations as part of my training in mechanistic modelling and energy analysis.

Computational Approach

I used Gaussian for geometry optimisation, transition-state searches and energy processing, and assembled energetic cycles to compare candidate pathways. Exact functional, basis-set, effective-core-potential and solvation settings are not specified in this public summary.

My Contribution

  • Participated in a team investigation comparing oxidative-addition, SET and SN2-type pathways.
  • Prepared molecular models, performed geometry optimisations and transition-state searches, and assembled energetic cycles and reaction-energy plots.
  • Repeatedly refined an oxidative-addition transition-state starting geometry and learned to distinguish numerical convergence problems from problems in the chemical model.
  • Carried out related cobalt-system calculations, including constrained optimisations, data processing and plotting, following a plan developed with more experienced members of the research team.

Mechanistic Challenge

Locating the oxidative-addition transition state required repeated adjustments to the starting geometry. I also learned to diagnose methyl-group oscillation during optimisation. These tasks gave me practical experience in troubleshooting transition-state searches rather than treating a failed optimisation as a mechanistic conclusion.

Results and Validation

The project gave me experience carrying a mechanistic calculation from molecular models to energetic cycles and pathway comparisons. This public summary does not assign a preferred pathway or report unpublished quantitative barriers.

Output

This work forms part of my undergraduate research experience; no journal publication is claimed here.
